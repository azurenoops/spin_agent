CSP portal product brief

The CSP portal is the provider workspace for a tenant: productize offerings, keep the recorded parent authorization defensible, and show whether each Mission Owner’s declared use is still inside that authorization’s scope.

It does not run the MO package. It does not issue, extend, or withdraw an ATO. Attachment is not inheritance. A subscription in the CSP tenant is hosting, not inheritance. A recorded decision is not package readiness or monitoring health.

OfferingsWhat MOs can attach to
Parent ATORecorded authorization the tenant rests on
Scope fitIn-scope, exception, or out of authorized scope
Job to be done. Prove that real tenant use still matches the authorized offering — to the AO, 3PAO, and the CSP ISSM — without reading every MO system package.

MO / Org portal brief — standalone vs attached, eMASS import, residual work against pinned inheritance.

In scope

Create, version, publish, deprecate, and retire offerings.

Maintain offering ATO facts, CSP evidence, and CSP POA&Ms.

Publish CRM and pinned inheritance releases.

Approve offering access (from MO Provider hosting) and see per-MO plus aggregate scope fit.

What-if attach, significant change, and ConMon of the parent ATO.

Out of scope here

MO system creation, eMASS import, and residual control work — MO portal.

Issuing the MO authorization decision — MO AO, not CSP admin.

Broad read of MO SSP / assessment content as a shortcut to oversight.

Cloud telemetry product (scans, SIEM). Reuse facts; do not become the sensor.

RMF bounds and CSP value

Expert review, 2026-10-09. The CSP portal is in RMF bounds and useful if it helps the CSP maintain their authorized offering and prove who inherited what. It is out of bounds the moment it issues, withdraws, or “scores” an ATO as if the portal were the Authorizing Official.

Chicken or egg. A subscription in the CSP tenant often exists first. That is environment, not inheritance. Inheritance is never first. Out-of-scope declared use can sit in a CSP sub and remain out of authorized scope.

IATT vs ATO (two authorization paths)

Conversation shorthand “ITT” is IATT (Interim Authorization to Test). IATT and ATO are two AO decisions. Evidence from an IATT may feed an ATO package. The IATT decision does not become the ATO.

IATTATO
AO questionMay this be tested, in this env, for this window?May this operate, with this data, for this mission?
Typical envDev / isolated testProd
DataTest / syntheticMission data
InheritanceTest CRM/release if recordedOperational inheritance release
Dev / Test / Prod are hosting. Three subscriptions (Flank Speed–style) are allocate/associate — not three ATOs. IATT scoped to Dev but executed in Test because Dev is locked is declared-use drift: IATT+Dev in scope (env); IATT+Test exception; IATT+Prod out of scope. A test inheritance release is not the prod release.

Scope-fit language (keep the screens, change the claim)

Fits / Exception / Invalidating is not a NIST control. There is no official score for “this customer invalidates us.” The ISSM question is: is declared use still inside the authorized scope we recorded?

UI labelRMF claimWhy
FitsIn-scope declared useEligible to inherit the pinned release
ExceptionCRM / boundary exceptionStill at IL; extra path, overlay, or skipped shared duty. Residual risk; needs a human
InvalidatingOut of authorized scopeUse exceeds IL, data type, or boundary. The recorded decision no longer covers that use. The ATO is not automatically void
Non-negotiable. Out-of-scope use does not auto-withdraw the parent ATO. Input is declared use + published authorized use, both pinned to a version/release — not the MO SSP, scans, or a peek at the mission package.

Fed/Civ, DoD, and CMMC

Shorthand “CCMC” is treated as CMMC (Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification) unless a named office or contract is specified. A CSP does not get one authorization for the government. Three programs reuse NIST controls but different AOs, packages, and customers.

Product rule. Model offerings per recorded authorization, not “the company is FedRAMP” as one ATO. Scope-fit is against that offering’s pinned release.
CSP offering (commercial product)
 ├── Fed/Civ: FedRAMP (P-ATO or agency ATO) — NIST SP 800-53 Low/Moderate/High
 ├── DoD: IL-authorized PA/ATO (IL2–IL6, SRG, often eMASS)
 └── CMMC: not a CSP ATO — DIB contractors must use a qualifying cloud if they process CUI there
WhoNeed from the CSPThey still own

Fed/Civ

JAB P-ATO or agency ATO is the offering authorization. The agency still ATOs its system. Inheritance is the FedRAMP CRM. Offering ConMon is FedRAMP ConMon, not every agency tenant’s subscriptions.

DoD

The same commercial product is usually a different boundary, overlay, and AO. FedRAMP Moderate does not equal IL4/IL5. DoD may leverage FedRAMP as evidence; that does not replace a DoD authorization decision. Missions inherit only in-scope services at the authorized IL.

CMMC

Certifies the contractor’s CUI environment, not the CSP’s 800-53 package. If the contractor uses a CSP for CUI, DFARS 252.204-7012 requires FedRAMP Moderate or DoD-defined FedRAMP equivalency (third-party evidence, not a slide that says “equivalent”). Confirm current DoD policy before recording equivalency; SPIN must not invent it. The CSP package is environment qualification, not the contractor’s CMMC certificate.

How to model it

Offering A: FedRAMP Moderate, civilian CRM. Offering B: IL5 Azure Gov, DoD CRM. Optional recorded fact: DFARS/CMMC cloud eligibility as stated in source.

MOs adopt one pinned release. Never: “FedRAMP exists, so IL5 declared use is in scope.” Never: “CMMC Authorized” on CSP home because a FedRAMP date exists.

Equivalency is confirm against current DoD policy, not something this portal issues.

Epic 6 — the CSP-specific value

Inventory is not enough. Each attached MO must produce an impact record: extra data types, higher impact level, extra interconnections, overlays, and customer configs outside the published CRM. Aggregate those records so the ISSM can see boundary creep before an annual review does.

Store — impact record. MO system, offering version, inherited release, declared use, exceptions, severity, and whether use still fits authorized scope.
Do not store — MO package body. Residual implementation statements, MO assessment results, and MO AO decision artifacts stay in the MO portal unless explicitly shared.
